## 1. Verdict

- **The method will survive its first real feature; the tooling around it will stall it in four named places** (§2): the merger's red-first check goes red for the wrong reason on every task that adds its own test file; the three-fix-cycle rule routes every design-review finding to the human because a design has no runnable check; the Stop gate will spin or be overridden while a workflow runs in the background; and the token cap and the `metrics` entry both read the main transcript, which the fan-out never writes to.
- **It is still too heavy for a medium feature, but the weight is in the scaffolding, not the principles**: ~14 agents, 5 gates plus a lint on every commit and a hook on every turn, 7 files, 11 entry kinds, 9 caps and about a dozen named rules. Half of that can go without losing a check (§3).
- **The three documents do not tell one story in six places** (§5): mode vocabulary (`full` vs `standard`), where a bug enters, whether criteria get fix cycles, whether a retro is mandatory, whether a predicate check exists, and who authors the proving check for a fix.
- **§16 builds all of Part A before anything runs.** A one-week slice with no new scripts would settle five of the seven bake-off unknowns first (§6).
- **The essay is good and nearly publishable**; its weakest paragraphs are the ones defending rulings rather than stating principles (§7).

## 6. Build order and the smallest proving slice

**§16 is not the cheapest path.** It builds templates, five scripts, a commit hook, agent frontmatter, five workflows, every skill rewrite, the gate and every cap, the metrics and the prune (§16:878-894) before one feature runs (§16:895). The record says the method already ran for three weeks on hand-kept files and native workflows (§A8.1:254). What is unproven is the native-feature mechanics in §A7, and most of §16's output does not touch them: `pd-new`, `pd-html`, `pd-status`, `pd-adopt`, the metrics, the prune and the retirements prove nothing about whether a worktree starts from head, a merger can merge it, or a gate can hold a `/goal` run.

**One week, no new scripts beyond ~150 lines, kill or prove:**

1. **Day 1.** Pick one real small feature in a real project. Hand-write README.md frontmatter, log.md and shape.md from the template shapes in Part B §5-§6; keep today's `phase-gate.sh`. Set `worktree.baseRef: "head"` in that project's settings. No pd-lint, no pd-new, no pd-html.
2. **Day 2.** Copy `followups-tasks-capped.js` into `.claude/workflows/implement.js`: drop the per-task review and QA, switch implementers to `isolation: 'worktree'` and `agentType` of a sonnet agent with the verification paragraph, add the merger with the repaired red/green (test files checked out onto the pre-merge tree), add the `budget.spent()` check. Keep `followups-phase-integration-generic.js` as `verify.js` with the loop bounded at three and QA owning the fixer's red/green. This settles §A7.1, §A7.2 and the merger hand-off.
3. **Day 3.** Write the Stop gate: 40 lines that read `## Now` (`goal:` and `run in flight:`), run `phase-gate.sh`, honour `stop_hook_active`, and sum tokens over every `*.jsonl` in the session's transcript directory. Run the feature under `/goal` in auto mode with allow rules for the agents' tools. This settles §A7.4 and §A7.6, and tells you whether the gate spins against a background workflow.
4. **Days 4-5.** Finish the feature by hand through Verify and Finish. Count: agents, human stops, tokens from the summed transcripts, wall-clock, fix cycles. Write the one `metrics` entry by hand.

**Kill criteria, stated before the week starts:** the worktree does not start from the feature branch; the merger cannot find or merge the task branches; the gate cannot hold a `/goal` run without spinning; the feature's tokens exceed the comparable hand-run feature by more than 2x. Any one of those means the native workflow path is wrong and `pd-lint`/`pd-status` would have been built for a process that does not run. If all four pass, §16 step 2 is then worth building, and `pd-html` first, as it says.

## 7. The essay as writing

**Would a thoughtful engineer who has never seen pd find it convincing?** Mostly yes. It makes an argument, not a list, and the argument is the right one: the author must not be the judge, and a check nobody runs is a preference. The three worked examples carry it.

**Strongest.**
- The opening claim (essay:3-5): a coding agent "will fill any gap you leave with something confident and plausible." That is the thesis and it is true.
- §3's three versions of one requirement (essay:86-103), and the sentence that the runnable-but-worthless one "is the interesting failure".
- §4's `retry(fn, budget)` contract against the prose brief (essay:116-145), and §7's cache example (essay:200-223).
- §14's record: "the gates caught all seven, and the written rule against it prevented none" (essay:376-378). The best sentence in the piece, and the one a sceptic cannot wave away.
- The closing list (essay:402-418) is a usable one-page method on its own.

**Weakest.**
- **§1's "converse" paragraph (essay:43-48)** reads as a defence of a ruling (S1) rather than a principle. A newcomer does not know what it is arguing against; it should either be an example ("design decides the shape of `retry`; the plan decides it ships before the caller that needs it") or move to §2 as a depth rule.
- **§11 is the longest section and the most hedged** (essay:291-324): six paragraphs, two of them about what the evidence does not show. Cut to: review once, fix by checks, three cycles as a chosen budget, a judgement finding needs a check, ask reviewers only for correctness. The placement paragraph (319-324) is good and should open the section.
- **§13 drifts into the plan** (essay:349-357): token budgets, fan-outs, finish lines a run "can talk itself past" are mechanisms, and the essay promised principles, not a pipeline (essay:19). Keep "stop rather than guess" and "a stop has to reach someone"; move the budget detail to the plan.
- **No express criterion of its own.** §2 names what forces full depth (essay:62-64) but never says when to skip. Claude Code's own line, already in the plan's sources, is the one to borrow: if you could describe the diff in one sentence, skip the plan. Still missing after two reviews.
- **Two sentences need repair:** essay:180 ("The saving is real, but it is not the reason to rely on, since...") and essay:112-114, where "Spec-driven development" is named and dismissed in one breath, which reads as a swipe.
- **Vocabulary.** "Pinned", "home", "predicate", "scaffolding" are used consistently and defined by use; fine. "Stage" in the essay is "phase" in the plan and the frontmatter; one word would help a reader who moves from one to the other.
